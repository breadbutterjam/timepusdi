About this Moon Phase Viewer

Everything here is calculated, not looked up from a fixed calendar. The app works out the Sun's and Moon's real positions for the exact date and time you're viewing, and derives the Moon's phase and the Gujarati tithi from the angle between them.

A tithi is one of 30 divisions of the lunar month, each covering 12° of that angle. Because a tithi rarely lines up neatly with a calendar day, one day can contain a tithi boundary partway through it. The gear icon in the detail view (tap the Moon to open it) lets you choose how "today's tithi" is picked:

  • Tithi at sunrise (default) — the traditional panchang convention: whichever tithi is active at sunrise in Mumbai.
  • Tithi with max hours — whichever tithi occupies the most of the 24-hour day.

The Moon photo is real NASA imagery for that exact date and time — not a generic phase icon — matched to the computed phase.

Tithi timings are precomputed for speed (currently 2024–2033) and calculated live on the fly for any date outside that range, so nothing breaks if you navigate far into the future or past.

Sun & Moon position formulas — adapted from Richard Fitzpatrick's An Introduction to the Almagest, a modern presentation of classical low-precision solar and lunar theory. Read the source

Sunrise times — computed with a vendored copy of SunCalc by Vladimir Agafonkin (BSD-2-Clause license).

Moon photography — NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's "Dial-A-Moon" series, real rendered imagery of the Moon's true phase and libration for a given hour. See the gallery

Tithi and Gujarati month names — traditional Hindu luni-solar (panchang) calendar conventions, hand-maintained rather than pulled from an external source.
